# Prototype: Tuesday (and Wednesday morning)

**Status: PROPOSAL.** Nothing here is approved. Names are placeholders by role. Items marked ❓ need the creator's decision.

## What the prototype must prove

1. Replaying the same Tuesday produces substantially different evenings.
2. A bad night makes the player curious about Wednesday, not eager to reload.
3. Characters remember Tuesday on Wednesday, and it shows.
4. His condition (drunk, tired, humiliated, confident) changes what he thinks, says and can do. It comes through the inner monologue, not numbers.
5. The same situation can play funny or tragic depending on what led there.

## Locations (kept to the minimum)

| Location | Use |
|---|---|
| **His apartment + stairwell** | Morning, the neighbour encounter, coming home at night |
| **The permits office** | Workday, Wednesday morning |
| **The party venue** | Evening. Proposal below ❓ |
| **The night** (one extra place) | After-party bar, or somewhere unexpected. Can be very simple. |

**Party venue (agreed: public place):** the riverside terrace of a new EU-funded restaurant. The Boss books it for **17:00 on a Tuesday** and hands out **two drink tickets per person**. The ticket system is the running joke: it collapses within the first hour. Everyone already knows the Secretary has collected tickets from the non-drinkers.

### Outsiders at the party (proposal)

Because the venue is public, each run draws **1-2 outsiders** from a small pool (seeded at the start). They can pull Office Man into a thread that has nothing to do with the office. This is cheap replay variety: the office cast stays the same, the strangers change. Pool ideas, all ❓ and to be shaped by the creator:

- **Western consultants** on a team-building night at the next table. They explain the country to whoever will listen. Satire aimed at the Western gaze.
- **A wedding or hen party** sharing the terrace. Loud, sentimental, and an open door for drunk choices.
- **The developer** behind a permit the office is handling, there "by coincidence."
- **Staff:** a bartender or waiter who sees the whole evening from the outside.
- **Someone from his past:** an ex, an old school friend who's done better or much worse.

Not every outsider thread needs to pay off on Wednesday. Some can simply return in chapter 2, or never.

## The day

**1. Morning: apartment (short)**
- He wakes alone. The inner monologue sets his voice and his mood today; the starting mood is lightly randomised.
- The neighbours are heard through the wall first: an argument, a door, music. The player meets the family as sound before seeing any of them.
- One encounter in the stairwell (see *Neighbours* below).

**2. Workday: the office**
- **The Secretary** is running the party and needs everyone's confirmation, a contribution for the gift, and a bit of attention.
- **The Boss** gives a short speech: "It's a Tuesday, people. Two tickets. We're professionals." He is charming and competent. Maybe he asks Office Man for something small that's slightly wrong.
- **The Veteran** fixes the case nobody else can. One small, easily missed strange moment (a hint, or nothing at all, depending on the playthrough).
- **The Awkward Colleague** wants to sit together at the party, or share a taxi. Easy to brush off, and it's remembered either way.
- **One work case** that matters on Wednesday: a permit decision with a person behind it. Ideally tied to someone he'll meet tonight. ❓ (The Neighbour's permit idea below could serve as this.)

**3. Evening: the party** (the core of the prototype)
- The Boss's Wife arrives, too dressed up, with a new bag. She knows everything about her husband, and so does everybody else.
- The tickets run out, and the night opens up.
- Key situations, available or not depending on his state and earlier choices:
  - The Secretary, drunk, hopeful about the Boss, then crushed; Office Man is nearby.
  - The Boss wants a wingman, or a witness who keeps quiet.
  - The Wife alone at the bar, too honest after the third glass.
  - The Veteran says something unsettling, or something kind.
  - The Awkward Colleague's moment: a speech, a song, or a dance that is either a disaster or briefly glorious.

**4. Night: after the party**
- Go home, follow colleagues to a bar, follow someone else, or get lost.
- Coming home drunk is its own scene: stairs, keys, the wrong door, the neighbours.

**5. Wednesday morning: consequences**
- Wake up somewhere: his bed, someone else's, a sofa, a police station or a hospital waiting room (but not dead).
- At the office, everyone remembers something, and not always correctly.
- Every major Tuesday thread gets at least one reaction here. The prototype ends with something that could lead on (a call, a summons, a message, an invitation).

## Five very different evenings (examples, not scripts)

1. **The clear head.** He leaves sober and early. On Wednesday he's the only one who remembers everything, and everyone wants to know what he saw. He becomes the keeper of secrets, or blackmail bait.
2. **The Secretary.** They end the night together. On Wednesday she's hopeful. Kindness, cowardice or cruelty: each choice shapes a long thread.
3. **The Boss's boy.** He becomes the Boss's wingman. Career doors open; he's now complicit, and the Wife saw it.
4. **The Wife.** A conversation that goes too far, in either direction. On Wednesday the Boss doesn't know. Yet.
5. **The disaster.** A fight, a fall, the river, or the police. He wakes up on the Awkward Colleague's sofa, and the Awkward Colleague made breakfast.

**Agreed: nobody dies in the prototype.** Deaths are revisited later.

## The neighbour (agreed: the man and the permit)

In the stairwell he's friendly and a little too familiar. He's built something on his balcony without a permit and knows exactly where Office Man works. A small favour: home and work collide, petty corruption, no clean answer. This doubles as the work case. The wife and daughter are only heard through the wall.

**The "fix it and you can marry my daughter" joke ❓.** It works only if the daughter is an adult. Proposal: she's mid-twenties and still lives at home (common in the region). Then the joke tells us exactly who he is, her mortification through the wall is funny, and the line sets up a possible later thread with an adult. If she's a teenager, the joke is off the table.

## Systems this tests (to be designed together)

- **Conditions:** intoxication (sober to wrecked), tiredness, mood. They change monologue, options and outcomes.
- **Traits:** a small hidden set (to be discussed, not invented here). Shown indirectly.
- **Memory:** each character stores what Office Man did on Tuesday and reacts on Wednesday.
- **Randomness:** seeded at the start of the run (starting mood, who's where when, small events). Reloading replays the same luck.
