# The Misadventures of Office Man: Game Vision

Owner and creative director: the project creator. This document records the creative vision. It is the reference for every design decision.

## How to read this document

Every item carries a status tag:

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[REQUIRED]** | Confirmed requirement. Do not change without explicit approval. |
| **[PREFERENCE]** | The creator's current preference. Strong default, open to a reasoned alternative. |
| **[EXAMPLE]** | Illustrative only. Not a prescribed storyline, mechanic or target. |
| **[OPEN]** | Unresolved. Must be discussed with the creator before it is decided. |
| **[AGREED]** | Decided together after discussion. Each entry records the date. |

Assistant suggestions are never recorded as [REQUIRED] or [AGREED] until the creator explicitly approves them.

## 1. Fundamental vision

- **[REQUIRED]** Mature, darkly humorous, narrative-driven adventure about an ordinary office worker in a large fictional city.
- **[REQUIRED]** He is not a hero, detective, chosen one or extraordinary person.
- **[REQUIRED]** No central mystery, no predetermined heroic journey, no world to save. The story is his life. Life happens.
- **[REQUIRED]** Lives must diverge radically. Not one story with a few alternative endings.
- **[REQUIRED]** Divergence comes from authored events, character interactions, player choices, hidden systems and controlled randomness.
- **[REQUIRED]** A disastrous life must be as narratively compelling as a successful one.
- **[REQUIRED]** The player should feel they are living a life, not completing a campaign.
- **[EXAMPLE]** One playthrough: career success, friendship, romance, growth. Another: unemployment, betrayal, addiction, crime, violence, early death.
- **[EXAMPLE]** A playthrough might last two hours or ten or more. These are not targets.

**Final principle:** The game is not about winning at life. It is about discovering what kind of life unfolds. Whatever happens, the player should want to know what happens next.

## 2. Core design principle: No optimal life

- **[REQUIRED]** The player should want to keep playing whether things go well or badly.
- **[REQUIRED]** Failure often creates a new situation, relationship, complication or opportunity, not a dead end.
- **[REQUIRED]** There is no universally correct life path.
- **[REQUIRED]** Do not achieve this mainly by restricting save/load. Make continuing more interesting than restarting.
- **[REQUIRED]** Some consequences may be irreversible. The game may end abruptly, including through death.
- **[REQUIRED]** Outcomes need not be equally positive, rewarding or long, but every playthrough should feel like a meaningful story.
- **[REQUIRED]** Avoid:
  - obvious good-versus-evil choices
  - clearly optimal dialogue options
  - visible success percentages that invite optimisation
  - arbitrary punishments disconnected from decisions
  - failure states that only force repetition
  - a single preferred ending disguised as freedom
- **Design test:** when something goes terribly wrong, does the player want to reload, or to continue? Design for continuing.
- **[EXAMPLE]** Impressing a manager brings promotion and money but damages personal relationships.
- **[EXAMPLE]** Embarrassing yourself at a company party leads to an unexpected friendship and a different life.
- **[EXAMPLE]** Losing a job is devastating, but opens a new career or social circle.
- **[EXAMPLE]** A seemingly good romantic choice has consequences that surface much later.
- **[EXAMPLE]** A terrible decision genuinely destroys his life.

## 3. The world

- **[REQUIRED]** A large, fictional city with contrasts: glamorous and seductive, ordinary, depressing, dangerous, absurd.
- **[PREFERENCE]** Offices, apartment buildings, restaurants, bars, nightclubs, shops, hotels, wealthy neighbourhoods, industrial areas, rundown districts.
- **[REQUIRED]** The city is not a backdrop for quests. Its people have lives, ambitions, relationships and problems of their own.
- **[REQUIRED]** Characters can change jobs, form relationships, have crises, disappear from his life and return.
- **[REQUIRED]** The world reacts to Office Man without revolving around him.
- **[REQUIRED]** Do not turn it into a crime thriller, detective story, supernatural mystery or conventional RPG by default.
- **[REQUIRED]** No overarching antagonist or central conflict is needed.
- **[OPEN]** Era, cultural identity, visual direction and structure of the city. The assistant may propose; the creator decides.
- **[PREFERENCE]** First thought: a made-up Eastern European country. All writing in English. The setting might be used to make fun of biases. Not settled.
- **[AGREED 2026-10-18]** Office Man is a local, not a foreigner.
- **[AGREED 2026-10-18]** Setting: a small, contemporary country that recently joined the EU. EU funding pours in; glass towers rise next to concrete housing blocks; Western consultants explain the country to itself. The permits office sits in the middle of the money flood. Present-day technology (smartphones, Teams, Instagram). Satire targets bias, especially the Western gaze, not the locals.
- **[AGREED 2026-10-27]** The currency is the euro: the country is new in the EU and has already switched. Teo's pay, payday and whether money shows on screen are still open (proposed: it doesn't; he checks his balance on the phone).
- **[PREFERENCE]** Names and signs use an invented language that blends several real ones, so no real country is the joke.
- **[PREFERENCE]** A warm, bright summer as a counterpoint to the grey-Eastern-Europe image.
- **[OPEN]** Country, city and character names.

## 4. Tone, humour and adult content

- **[REQUIRED]** Combines: dark and absurd humour; everyday workplace situations; adult relationships and sexuality; friendship, romance, intimacy; ambition, success, failure; crime, corruption, moral ambiguity; addiction, betrayal, self-destruction; genuine tragedy; kindness, happiness, warmth.
- **[REQUIRED]** Moves naturally between comedy and tragedy. Not relentlessly bleak, not everything a joke.
- **[REQUIRED]** Humour comes mainly from characters, circumstances, dialogue and unexpected consequences.
- **[REQUIRED]** For adults. Consensual sex, nudity, desire, affairs and complicated adult relationships may be meaningful parts of the experience.
- **[REQUIRED]** Sexual exploitation, coercion and sexual violence may appear only as serious narrative subjects with consequences. Never as titillation, never as player-controlled sexual activity.
- **[REQUIRED]** Sexuality is one aspect of a believable adult world, not the main mechanic or gimmick.
- **[REQUIRED]** Do not sanitise unnecessarily. Do not include disturbing material for shock value.
- **[REQUIRED]** Minors (e.g. the neighbours' teenage daughter) are never part of any sexual or romantic content, storyline or player option. Other characters may say inappropriate things about them only when the scene clearly frames it as wrong.

### Tone references [AGREED 2026-10-18]

These are tonal references, not templates to imitate.

- **Primary:** Disco Elysium (dark humour, flawed humanity, self-deception, memorable failures, surprising consequences). *After Hours* (1985) (an ordinary office worker in escalating absurd and dangerous situations). *Peep Show* (social embarrassment, selfishness, sexual awkwardness, terrible decisions, self-rationalisation). *A Serious Man* (2009) (the tragic absurdity of ordinary life falling apart).
- **Secondary:** *Fargo* (ordinary people, disastrous decisions, humour and darkness side by side). *The Office* (UK) (painfully believable workplace interactions). *Leisure Suit Larry* (adult humour and sexual misadventure, without its dated stereotypes). *A Confederacy of Dunces* (eccentric personalities, character-driven chaos). *Factotum* (flawed adulthood, unstable work, alcohol, sex, self-destruction).
- **[REQUIRED]** No constant jokes, no exaggerated cartoon personalities, no relentlessly cynical world.
- **[REQUIRED]** Humour emerges from human behaviour, social misunderstanding, bad judgment, coincidence and consequences.
- **[REQUIRED]** The same situation can be funny in one playthrough and tragic in another, depending on what led there.
- **[REQUIRED]** Office Man stays an ordinary, believable person in a world that can turn extraordinarily absurd without warning.

## 5. Characters

- **[REQUIRED]** Characters are the heart of the game.
- **[REQUIRED]** The creator has many character ideas. Do not invent and permanently establish the cast before the creator has contributed.
- **[REQUIRED]** Each significant character has: a recognisable personality; ambitions and motivations; strengths, flaws and contradictions; relationships with others; a life beyond Office Man; memory of meaningful interactions; the potential to change.
- **[REQUIRED]** Relationships develop through events, conversations and shared experiences.
- **[REQUIRED]** Characters are not quest dispensers or romance targets.
- **[REQUIRED]** Some become friends, partners, rivals or enemies. Some appear briefly and leave a lasting impression.
- **[REQUIRED]** Not everyone has a dramatic secret. Sometimes a person is just a person.

### Character drafts (creator's, 2026-10-18)

Status: creator's first sketches. Names depend on the setting. Nothing here is final.

- **Office Man.** "Office Man" is the game's title only; he has a real name in-game (not yet chosen). A local. His behaviour is shaped by the player's choices. Lives alone: no wife, no kids. Works for a state branch, probably city planning (building permits and similar). About 32 at the start. His midlife crisis can be the main theme of chapter 2. **[OPEN]** Name and backstory.
- **The Veteran (colleague).** A woman. Has worked there forever. Knows everything. Superb at the job. Secretly an extremely violent person, "a Unabomber waiting to happen".
  - **[AGREED]** The violence surfaces in very few playthroughs. Otherwise she's just a regular woman at work. Small hints are allowed. Never a mystery to solve.
- **The Awkward Colleague.** Socially awkward. Tries to be friendly but is just weird. Not a main character. **[AGREED]** Design role: the person who's still there when everything else has collapsed; a possible way back for a ruined Office Man.
- **The Neighbours.** The family next door: a man, his wife, their daughter. On Tuesday Office Man meets the man in the stairwell. The man has built something without a permit, knows where Office Man works, and asks a favour. The wife and daughter are only heard through the wall.
  - **[AGREED]** The wife, **Dana**: the opposite of her daughter. Modest, slim, worn out, because nobody in the family does any housework except her. (Creator, 2026-10-19.)
  - **[AGREED]** The daughter, **Lenka** (16-18, 17 in the script): looks very much like Stojan, a female version of him. Annoying, self-centred, believes she deserves to be rich but won't do anything to get there; waiting to marry someone rich. (Creator, 2026-10-19.) Her plan is about her future as an adult; she is never part of romantic or sexual content (§4).
- **The Boss's Wife.** About 50, same as the Boss. Used to be attractive; age has worked against her. They have money, and she has become a typical Eastern European new-money woman who overcompensates. She knows about his affairs very well, and swallows her pride with money and handbags.
- **The Boss.** About 50. Good-looking and knows it, "big dick aura". Married. Constantly sleeps around. Thinks nobody knows; everybody knows. Really great at his job, a genuinely good boss. Macho. Thinks his wife is too old.
- **The Secretary (Magda).** 21. Two children, aged 5 and 2, by different men; both live with their fathers. This is the root of her darker personality and angst. Overweight. Desperate for attention: low self-esteem, and she seeks gratification through sex. Sleeps with anyone who says yes, and cries when they dump her. Probably an alcoholic. Kind. Wants the Boss badly; he has never slept with her (in his eyes she's "too fat"). Her darker personality will be developed much more later in the game.
  - **[REQUIRED]** We never make fun of her. We laugh with her, and we feel for her tragic life.

## 6. Dialogue and narrative systems

- **[REQUIRED]** All dialogue is authored in advance.
- **[REQUIRED]** No generative AI, language-model API or online inference at runtime. The game works fully offline.
- **[REQUIRED]** Conversations should not feel like rigid, predictable dialogue trees.
- **[REQUIRED]** Options and outcomes are influenced by: personality and abilities; temporary physical and emotional conditions; previous decisions; relationships and reputation; what each character knows or believes; the current situation; controlled randomness.
- **[REQUIRED]** Avoid an architecture where every outcome needs a separately authored storyline.
- **[REQUIRED]** Avoid meaningless procedural variation that produces incoherent stories. The goal is believable causality, not randomness for its own sake.

## 7. Hidden character development

- **[PREFERENCE]** Underlying characteristics that shape his experiences, loosely like RPG attributes.
- **[REQUIRED]** Do not copy the presentation or mechanics of any particular existing game.
- **[EXAMPLE]** Possible traits: confidence, judgment, social ability, self-control, physical coordination. Not a final list.
- **[REQUIRED]** Influences can change him temporarily or permanently.
- **[EXAMPLE]** Alcohol raises confidence and lowers coordination and judgment. Humiliation lowers confidence for a long time. Repeated social success changes how he approaches people. Stress, fatigue, illness or intoxication change available choices. Major life events permanently change his personality.
- **[REQUIRED]** The system should make players understand him as a person, not optimise him as numbers.
- **[AGREED 2026-10-18]** Personality is a set of two-sided tensions, not scores: both ends help and hurt, so there is no "maxed" Teo. Working set: **Nerve** (bold ↔ careful), **Warmth** (open ↔ closed), **Appetite** (hungry ↔ held back), **Honesty** (blunt ↔ smooth). Names use plain English. Details: `design/traits_proposal.md`.
- **[AGREED 2026-10-18]** Hidden: no numbers on screen. Personality shows through Teo's inner voice, which choices appear and how they're worded, what people say, and hindsight lines naming causes.
- **[AGREED 2026-10-18]** Teo's baseline: careful, open, held back, smooth, a man who lets life happen to him. He starts tired and jaded from government work.
- **[AGREED 2026-10-19]** Starting values: the creator's baseline Teo, with one or two traits nudged a step at random per life. Always Teo, never quite the same man. No character-creation screen.
- **[AGREED 2026-10-19]** Lasting change is rare: a trait shifts a step only after a big moment the player saw (the first time he stands up to Viktor, a night he can't remember, a public humiliation). Day to day, conditions (drink, tiredness, mood) push him instead.
- **[AGREED 2026-10-19]** At the end of the day, a few plain lines say who Teo was today. No numbers.
- **[AGREED 2026-10-26]** Lasting change is rarer: at most one shift a day, and the common moments need a second sign (humiliated twice in an evening; the favour delivered or got away with; filming Kornel and then pretending nothing happened; standing by Kornel and it working).
- **[AGREED 2026-10-19]** Four traits for now. Pride (can't lose face ↔ can laugh at himself) only later, if a story needs it.

## 8. Gameplay and interaction

- **[PREFERENCE]** Narrative adventure: exploration, dialogue, interaction, meaningful decisions. Exact format open to recommendation.
- **[PREFERENCE]** Interests: exploring detailed urban environments; memorable characters; examining objects and places; finding opportunities through conversation; choices with uncertain consequences; unexpected developments; radically different lives across playthroughs.
- **[REQUIRED]** No combat, inventory puzzles, skill checks or other RPG conventions unless they clearly improve the experience. Every system must justify itself.
- **[REQUIRED]** Challenge unnecessary complexity.
- **[REQUIRED]** The game must stay entertaining during an ordinary day, not only during extraordinary events.
- **[AGREED 2026-10-18]** Time structure: hybrid. 3-4 chapters years apart, each a few days played in detail. Between chapters, a playable "years pass" sequence of quick moments and small choices. A life can end in any chapter.
- **[AGREED 2026-10-18]** Narration: Office Man's inner monologue.
- **[AGREED 2026-10-18]** Opening: a Tuesday with the office summer party that evening. The Boss scheduled it on a weekday so nobody would drink. Everyone gets hammered anyway.
- **[AGREED 2026-10-18]** Gameplay format, modelled on the creator's earlier prototype *Deadhouse* (`E:\Dev\RPG`): a **pixel-art world with direct control** (arrows/WASD/controller), side-on view with depth, small pixel characters visible in the scene, walk up to people and objects and interact. Spoken lines float above characters. **Conversations open over the live scene**: a large illustrated portrait bottom-left, text and choices bottom-right. No combat. (A visual-novel-style dialogue screen was built first by mistake; it is being rearranged into this.)
- **[AGREED 2026-10-26]** The people at the party follow plans of their own, whether Teo is there or not. At most one person comes to Teo on their own per evening. The party runs 17:00 to 01:00.
- **[PREFERENCE 2026-10-18]** No visible clock. Time exists in the engine (events need it) but is felt, not read: the world changes (light, people leaving), Teo's monologue notices, clocks are objects you can look at, and arrival cards say "Tuesday · evening". Time moves only when Teo does something; walking is free. Proposed by the assistant after the creator asked whether time needs displaying; pending objection.

## 9. Visual direction

- **[PREFERENCE]** Expressive pixel art. Open to alternatives with a convincing artistic and technical case.
- **[REQUIRED]** Large, expressive character portraits in dialogue. Faces, emotions, personality, reactions.
- **[REQUIRED]** Supports both absurd comedy and dark or intimate scenes.
- **[PREFERENCE]** Detailed environments, meaningful facial expressions, character animation and body language, atmospheric lighting, distinctive locations, strong consistency, a polished intentional style.
- **[OPEN]** Rendering resolution and production technique.
- **[AGREED 2026-10-18]** Portrait development is **paused**. Final character art is decided much later. Strongest references so far, preserved in `art/reference/` with their source code: `drawn_flat.png` (flat illustration) and `construct_1_animation.png` (animation-inspired construction). The creator preferred the animation approach, made more mature.
- **[AGREED 2026-10-18]** Until then, existing portraits are temporary art. The dialogue interface must support large portraits, multiple expressions per character, and replacing artwork without changes to the dialogue system.

## 10. Asset production

- **[REQUIRED]** Every creative asset is made specifically for this project.
- **[REQUIRED]** No third-party asset packs, stock art, downloaded sprites, existing game graphics, copyrighted music or other externally sourced creative assets.
- **[PREFERENCE]** Any self-contained technique is allowed: procedural graphics, code-constructed pixel art, vector/layered illustration, runtime rendering, shaders and lighting, generated spritesheets and animation, procedural textures, synthesised music and sound.
- **[REQUIRED]** Libraries, engines, fonts and dependencies are allowed when their licences permit commercial use. Licensing obligations must be identified.
- **[REQUIRED]** Generated content should be reproducible and maintainable where practical.
- **[REQUIRED]** The goal is a beautiful, distinctive, enjoyable game, not a demonstration of code-generated graphics.

## 11. Technical constraints

- **[REQUIRED]** Runs on Windows.
- **[REQUIRED]** Works fully offline. No AI services, no paid external APIs required by players.
- **[REQUIRED]** Technology stack chosen on merit. No engine is assumed correct.
- **[REQUIRED]** Consider save/load, accessibility, localisation readiness and robust narrative state.
- **[REQUIRED]** Prefer maintainable architecture over technical sophistication.
- **[REQUIRED]** The creator approves the technical recommendation before development begins.
- **[AGREED 2026-10-18]** Technology stack delegated to the assistant: Godot 4 (.NET/C#), ink for scene dialogue, and a custom C# life engine (world state, storylets, NPC arcs, memory, director). Headless simulation for testing.
- **[AGREED 2026-10-18]** An art test (one portrait with several expressions plus one lit room) runs alongside the text prototype to validate code-built pixel art early.

## 12. Ownership and public identity

- **[REQUIRED]** Independently owned by the creator. Presented under the creator's name or a studio name they choose.
- **[REQUIRED]** The AI assistant is a development tool, not an author, publisher, studio or copyright holder.
- **[REQUIRED]** No AI credits, branding, splash screens, watermarks, signatures or AI references in the game world (unless the creator asks).
- **[REQUIRED]** Applicable law, licences, platform disclosure rules and contracts take precedence. Required AI disclosures (e.g. on storefronts) must be identified and complied with, never falsified.
- **[OPEN]** Studio name.

## 13. Development philosophy

- **[REQUIRED]** Iterative development. Prove the core experience before expanding content.
- **[REQUIRED]** No large city or extensive story before the core experience works.
- **[PREFERENCE]** First milestone: one playable day in Office Man's life.
- **[EXAMPLE]** Prototype contents: a small apartment, an office, a bar or restaurant, a handful of memorable characters, interconnected situations, hidden attributes and temporary conditions, relationships that remember, meaningfully different outcomes, several ways the day can end.
- **[REQUIRED]** The same starting situation should produce substantially different experiences, and make the player curious to replay.
- **[OPEN]** The assistant may recommend a different prototype if it better tests the central design challenges.

## 14. Collaboration

- **[REQUIRED]** Active partnership. The assistant challenges ideas, names weak or expensive ones, and proposes improvements.
- **[REQUIRED]** Distinguish creative decisions (creator), technical decisions (assistant), and shared design questions (together).
- **[REQUIRED]** Ask only about decisions that shape the game's identity, structure or experience. Take responsibility for technical execution.
- **[REQUIRED]** The creator contributes characters, situations, humour and narrative ideas throughout. Do not finalise large parts of the world without them.

## Current priority [AGREED 2026-10-18]

**The game experience comes first. Final artwork can wait.** The focus is the narrative simulation:

- hidden character attributes and temporary conditions
- relationships and persistent character memories
- authored dialogue with conditional choices and outcomes
- controlled randomness
- events triggered by circumstances and previous decisions
- consequences that create new opportunities and storylines
- multiple substantially different life trajectories

Measured against *No Optimal Life*: after a disaster, the player should want to continue out of curiosity, not reload.
Target: the first playable prototype, one day with few locations and characters but genuinely different outcomes.
Plan: `design/milestone_1.md` (approved 2026-10-18). The story and cast are not expanded without the creator.
